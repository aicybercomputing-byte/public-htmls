September 25, 2026 · 13:00:00 · ENG 4AI+X FeaturedRevisiting the Effectiveness of LLM Pruning for Test-Time Scaling

Large Language Models (LLMs) now exhibit remarkable growth in reasoning capabilities through test-time scaling (TTS). TTS uses additional compute during inference by generating thinking tokens. While TTS has significantly enhanced the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, these improvements come at the cost of increased inference time and computational demands. Model pruning provides a promising way to reduce these computational requirements by removing redundant parameters from the model. However, previous work has shown that naively pruning reasoning models can lead to severe degradation in performance. In this talk, I will show that, unlike structured pruning approaches that past work has explored in the context of reasoning and TTS, unstructured pruning methods can retain reasoning performance comparable to the full model, and at times even exceeding it. I will conclude by discussing what these findings reveal about reasoning models and their broader implications for building more efficient AI systems without sacrificing capability.

The persuasiveness of AI’s evolving technological promise has shaped discourses, expectations, and disillusion towards the present and future of musical creation. February 13, 2026AI+X FeaturedAdvancing Controllable Image Generation with Consistency and Preference Optimization

This presentation will explore recent advances in controllable image generation using diffusion models, with a focus on achieving precise alignment between generated images and structured visual conditions such as segmentation masks, edges, depth maps, and human poses. The talk will begin by outlining the limitations of existing conditional diffusion methods, which often lack explicit objectives to enforce fine-grained controllability. I will then introduce two recent approaches that address this challenge through consistency- and preference-based learning. The first leverages pixel-level cycle consistency and efficient reward fine-tuning to strengthen adherence to input controls without incurring the high computational cost of full diffusion backpropagation. The second reformulates controllable generation as a condition-level preference optimization problem, enabling robust optimization across all diffusion timesteps. Together, these methods demonstrate significant improvements in controllability while preserving image quality, highlighting new directions for reliable and scalable control in generative vision models.

February 20, 2026AI+X FeaturedGeospatial Understanding of Foundation Models: Self-Supervision, Robustness, and Resource-Constrained Learning

Large-scale geospatial understanding from satellite imagery remains less mature than natural-image vision. Current practice often relies on narrow, task-specific pipelines, and there is limited work on truly task-agnostic foundation models that remain robust under domain shift, geographic variation, and diverse real-world scenes. We advance remote-sensing foundation-model development and evaluation along three axes. First, we build self-supervised multispectral models for satellite image classification by adapting common SSL methods (BYOL, Barlow Twins, DINO, MoCo, SimSiam) to multispectral inputs. We pretrain on EuroSAT and BigEarthNet and evaluate cross-dataset transfer. The resulting multispectral checkpoints, especially those trained on BigEarthNet achieve strong transfer despite using only a fraction of the data typically used in ImageNet-scale training. Second, we benchmark eight ViT-based geospatial foundation models (including SatMAE/Scale-MAE/Cross-Scale MAE, the Geospatial Foundation Model, and supervised multi-task pretraining) via end-to-end fine-tuning on four downstream tasks: classification, segmentation, oriented object detection, and change detection. Performance is highly task-dependent: models are competitive for classification and segmentation but underperform for change detection and oriented detection, and cross-dataset generalization degrades when imagery comes from different satellites. Across experiments, pretraining data quality (spatial resolution and scene diversity) matters more than dataset size, and scaling from ViT-Base to ViT-Large yields ~1% gains. Finally, we introduce Hierarchical Attention Distillation (HAD) to transfer multi-scale spatial and semantic representations from a large teacher to a compact student using unlabeled data, reducing parameters to less than 5% of state-of-the-art solutions while retaining competitive performance.

Current augmented reality (AR) headset applications often rely on predefined gesture sets that fail to align with users' natural expectations, leading to unintuitive experiences. To address this gap, we conducted two separate open-ended elicitation studies with 30 adults and 20 children (ages 9-12). Participants were tasked with replicating 17 different actions (e.g., moving, rotating) on a 3D virtual cube using any interaction modality they preferred.ï¿½Our findings reveal critical differences and similarities across both participant groups. Adults predominantly default to direct hand manipulation (comprising 82%ï¿½of first choices) , but demonstrate a strong preference for interaction flexibility, with 52%ï¿½of secondary proposals switching to a different modality. Conversely, children expect to engage their entire bodies (e.g., kicking, running) and frequently incorporate external physical or imagined objects as tools. Furthermore, children distinctively construct narratives around their interactions to contextualize abstract tasks. By comparing these distinct mental models, this talk highlights the necessity of moving beyond static, unimodal gesture sets. We will discuss actionable design recommendations for future AR headset applications, emphasizing the need for multimodal flexibility, whole-body tracking, and physics-aware virtual environments to accommodate the diverse, intuitive expectations of all users.

Conversational artificial intelligence (C-AI) has the potential to scale speech-language therapy, yet current systems often fail when exposed to the variability and error patterns characteristic of disordered speech. Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being integrated into hardware design verification workflows, particularly for SystemVerilog Assertion (SVA) generation. This gap introduces risks of hallucinated or vacuous assertions, reducing confidence and increasing debugging effort, especially in the absence of golden RTL. Bridging this gap requires a method that enforces semantic alignment throughout the generation process rather than a post-generation step.ï¿½This talk will begin with a review of the current state of the art SVA generation methodologies and their limitations. To address these challenges, we present SpecAlign, a framework for evaluating and refining LLM-generated SVAs with a novel approach that explicitly enforces semantic alignment between assertions and the specification. SpecAlign iteratively refines and provides evidence for natural language properties and SVAs with the goal of improving consistency, explainability, and confidence. This is achieved with two evidence- and reasoning-based loops that classify properties and SVAs based on semantic alignment and generate structured feedback and evidence. Additionally, SpecAlign provides a quantitative alignment score to measure semantic alignment across iterations, providing an alternative to traditional evaluation methods that rely on formal verification and golden RTL. The talk concludes with a discussion of future research directions in LLM-based SVA generation and the broader role of LLMs in hardware verification.

April 24, 2026AI+X FeaturedFrom Scaling to Safety: How Test-Time Compute Introduces a New Attack Surface in LLMs

Large Language Models (LLMs) are a key part of modern artificial intelligence, used in tasks ranging from complex reasoning to everyday interactions. As demand for better performance grows, they are made smarter through scaling, using more data, larger models, and more recently, Test-Time Scaling (TTS). TTS uses extra compute at inference time to generate better responses. While this improves output quality and reasoning, its safety risks are still not well understood. In this talk, I will explain how LLMs work, how scaling improves them, and why these improvements can also introduce new risks. I will begin with an overview of LLMs and scaling methods, focusing on TTS approaches such as Best-of-N. These methods can be understood through three main components: how responses are generated, how they are scored, and how much computation is used. Next, I will connect traditional adversarial attacks on LLMs to new risks introduced by TTS. I will then present our work, ï¿½Less Diverse, Less Safeï¿½, which identifies a new failure mode related to diversity in generated responses. I will show that this safety concern arises across different TTS methods and models and demonstrate that the threat can be transferred to closed-source systems and can bypass strong safety guardrails. I conclude by highlighting the need for safety-aware TTS design as an important step toward building safer and more reliable AI systems.

September 11, 2026CommunityBeyond Data: Imperfect Physics as a Prior for Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning (RL) promises agents that can improve through experience using only the supervision provided by a reward function. In robotics, however, real-world interaction is expensive, dense rewards are difficult to specify, and sparse rewards often lead to sample-inefficient learning. In this talk, I explore whether imperfect physics priors can nevertheless provide useful structure for RL. Focusing on goal-conditioned RL (GCRL) for robot navigation and manipulation, I show how control-theoretic principles can serve as inductive biases for policy learning. We first connect goal-reaching value functions to time-to-go fields and interpret the Eikonal equation as a simplified surrogate for the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, motivating Eikonal-informed value learning for challenging navigation problems. We then study its interaction with the quasimetric structure typical of GCRL. Their combination reveals the limitations of imposing these priors globally and motivates a hierarchical formulation. This becomes particularly important in contact-rich manipulation, where hybrid dynamics induce state-dependent controllability and motivate applying physics-informed structure only to appropriate state representations.

September 18, 2026 · 13:00:00 · ENG 4AI+X FeaturedCurvature-Weighted Capacity Allocation: A Minimum Description Length Framework for Layer-Adaptive Large Language Model Optimization

As large language models scale to trillions of parameters, hardware constraints on memory and compute become severe physical bottlenecks. A key driver of this inefficiency is that neural network layers are not created equal: some layers do the heavy lifting to reduce errors, while others are nearly redundant. Existing methods attempt to manage this, but they rely on gradient-based trial-and-error heuristics that ignore the local "curvature" of the learning landscape. These heuristics offer no guarantees and are computationally expensive, leading to a suboptimal distribution of resources and performance. To solve this, we take inspiration from information theory, especially the Minimum Description Length principle, to design a framework that acts as a mathematically rigorous budget manager for LLMs and any other AI models. By calculating a curvature-adjusted score for each layer, our system assesses a layer's true reducible risk and its actual potential to improve the model. It then uses a single convex optimization program to automatically drive two capacity allocation decisions: allocating extra computing capacity to the high-performing layers or pruning away wasted parameters from the low-performing ones. Tested on models like Mistral-7B and Gemma-7B, with benchmarks which include CoLA, MRPC, CommonsenseQA, etc. This budget-feasible approach replaces empirical guesswork with provable optimization guarantees, maximizing efficiency strictly within a global hardware limit.
